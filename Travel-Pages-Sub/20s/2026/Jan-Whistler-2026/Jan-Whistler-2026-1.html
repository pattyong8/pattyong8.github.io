Noah's Bday in Whistler

  • With Seattle Friends
  • January 8th-10th, 2026
  • Whistler, British Columbia

Noah does a birthday ski trip every year, and this year was no different. It was full of friends, food, drinks, and enjoying one of Noah's favorite sports. This was the first time we had gone to Whistler for his birthday, and boy was it a lavish and luxurious time. Good vibes all around.

I flew into Vancouver and had not been through the airport in a while. I had completely forgotten how cool it was inside, with the huge indoor water feature and all of the native architecture, boats, and artwork throughout the airport.

After making it through customs, I hopped on a bus for the roughly two and a half to three hour ride from Vancouver to Whistler. The bus was actually really comfortable, and eventually I made it to Whistler and checked into the hotel room I was sharing with Noah and Stephanie.

That night, Noah and I went to dinner with Logan at Bearfoot Bistro. This was actually the first time I ever met Logan, which is funny looking back on it now. Bearfoot Bistro is one of the nicer restaurants in Whistler, so we went all out with a bunch of different dishes to share and some really nice wine. It was definitely an expensive meal, but the food was great and felt like an appropriate way to kick off Noah's birthday weekend.

We also got to go down into the wine cellar and see their massive wine collection. Someone showed us around and explained that they even had an option where you could saber a bottle of champagne. Definitely one of the more extravagant ways to start a ski trip.

Our first real ski day started with Sebastian, Donnie, Noah, and me heading up the mountain. I had bought a two day pass and was expecting to get a ton of skiing in over the weekend. Turns out I bit off a little more than I could chew almost immediately.

For basically my first real run of the season, I decided to follow the guys down one of the steepest runs I had ever seen. I remember standing at the top, peering over the edge and thinking that this looked less like a ski run and more like a cliff. Noah went first, made a few turns, and successfully got himself down. Naturally, I decided I could do the same.

My first turn went well. I slid down quite a bit, made a second turn, and successfully got myself past a grouping of rocks. Then I went for my third turn, turned way too hard, and my skis went out from underneath me.

I started falling backwards down the mountain at an insane speed. I probably slid a couple hundred feet in a giant plume of snow and genuinely had no idea where I was, how fast I was going, or what was around me. I was doing everything I could to slow myself down until I eventually came to a stop. Noah and Donnie thought I might have died.

It was probably one of the scariest moments I have ever had skiing and a very good reminder that skiing is dangerous and you have to know your limits.

Once I collected myself, I realized I thankfully was not seriously injured. My left ankle was throbbing, though, and skiing on it was noticeably harder. I carefully made my way down the rest of the mountain, and we did a little more skiing before deciding it was probably time for a break.

We met up with the rest of the crew for lunch at the Four Seasons. Apparently, lunch here has become something of a tradition for this group. The restaurant is tucked inside the resort, and everyone seems to love stopping here in the middle of a ski day. The food was good, although nothing incredible, but it was nice to get the whole group together.

I kept telling Noah that I had paid for a ski pass and wanted to get my money's worth. My ankle also was not feeling too terrible at that point, so I decided to head back out with the guys.

We took the gondola all the way back up and ultimately only got one more run in. Given what had happened earlier, I was not taking any chances. I stuck almost entirely to groomers and carefully skied all the way back down to the bottom.

After skiing, we went to Scandinave, and initially my ankle actually felt okay.

I think this is where I really messed up.

At Scandinave, I kept going from extreme heat to extreme cold, jumping from the sauna into the ice bath and then doing it all over again. Whatever I had pulled or injured earlier clearly did not appreciate that decision. Suddenly, I was in some of the worst pain I had felt in a long time. I could barely put any weight on my foot, and even sitting still hurt.

I eventually took some painkillers, and the hotel was nice enough to give me crutches. Once the painkillers kicked in, I was at least able to survive the rest of the night.

Thankfully, we had a pretty great dinner waiting for us.

That evening, Noah had signed us up for dinner at Wild Blue. It was more of a prix fixe tasting experience, with shared appetizers, individual dishes, and plenty of wine. Even while hobbling around on crutches, I thought it was fantastic.

As good as Bearfoot Bistro had been the night before, I actually liked Wild Blue even more. The seafood, bread, wine, and dishes all felt a little more elegant and were incredibly tasty. Both restaurants were excellent, but Wild Blue was probably my favorite meal of the two.

Not exactly how I expected my first full ski day in Whistler to end, but at least I injured myself somewhere with very good food.

Because of my ankle, I decided not to ski on our last full day. It was definitely a bummer considering I had bought a two day pass, but that is what happens when you injure yourself on basically your first run of the trip.

It actually ended up being a pretty good day to sit out. It was snowing lightly throughout Whistler, which was beautiful from the perspective of hanging out and watching from the village. Apparently, visibility up on the mountain was terrible, though, so I was secretly pretty happy not to be skiing. Those are definitely not my favorite conditions.

My ankle was feeling better, although still nowhere near 100 percent, so I took it easy at the hotel and got ready for the main event of the day: Longhorns.

I headed over around 2 or 3 p.m., and we had such a good time. In classic Noah fashion, he had reserved a huge table right in front of the DJ booth. There was a pretty hefty minimum spend, but boy was it worth it. Our table even had a little fireplace in the middle, which made the whole setup even more vibey.

Ty, Kells, and I were some of the first people there, so I got to spend some quality time with them before everyone else finished skiing. We immediately got started on the food and drinks. Wings, nachos, oysters, pitchers of beer, and pretty much anything else we could order. Eventually, the rest of the group came down from the mountain and joined us, and that was when the real partying started.

Before everyone drank a little too much, I decided we needed to order at least one bottle service type item for Noah's birthday. We went with the cheapest bottle they had, which was a bottle of Veuve champagne, mostly because I wanted Noah to get the full birthday treatment. They came over with sparklers and a sign, and we took a ton of photos and videos celebrating Noah in very Noah fashion.

As the sun started going down, Longhorns became more and more of a party. The umbrellas came down, everyone started standing on chairs, the music got louder, and the entire place was going off. People around us kept ordering champagne showers, which were hilarious to watch, and the energy just kept building throughout the night.

Longhorns definitely has a reputation, and it completely lived up to it. More importantly, I got to spend some really good quality time with a lot of the people who had come out to celebrate Noah.

The next morning was significantly less glamorous.

I somehow woke up at 4 a.m., completely delirious and more than a little hungover, and realized I had no idea where my phone or wallet were. I also had not packed anything, my skis were still downstairs, and I had a bus leaving for Vancouver at 7 a.m.

Thank goodness I randomly woke up when I did because, without my phone, I did not even have an alarm to wake me up. Somehow, within about an hour, I packed all of my stuff, retrieved my skis, found both my phone and wallet, gathered everything I needed, and got myself ready to leave. I even managed to get another hour of sleep before heading out at 7 a.m.

If I had not randomly woken up at 4, I almost certainly would have missed the bus and probably my flight home too. Boy was that a tough travel day, but completely worth it for such a fun birthday trip with Noah and the gang.
