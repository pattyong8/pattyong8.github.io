Summer in Whistler

  • With Noah, Danny, and Sabs
  • August 19th-24th, 2026
  • Whistler, British Columbia

About a month after Danny and Sabrina got married, the two of them, along with Noah and me, headed up to Whistler for a wonderful summer weekend in the mountains. We golfed at the Fairmont, relaxed at Scandinave, had lunch at the Four Seasons, and even got to rock climb in Squamish. What a great way to spend some quality time with people I care about in one of the most beautiful places in the Pacific Northwest. The theme of the trip became clear: CAD is free.

Noah and I decided to drive up early so we could have some extra time in Whistler, work from our Airbnb, and hopefully get a little golf practice in before playing with Danny. The drive itself ended up being one of the highlights. I don't think I've ever seen Noah so excited about a drive. We passed beautiful water, enormous granite cliffs, and some incredible scenery as we made our way farther into British Columbia. Even though we still had to work along the way, it was a pretty great way to spend the day.

The border crossing was also entertaining. We felt like we were getting scrutinized a little more than necessary with all the questions, but eventually we made it through and continued north.

Getting there early ended up being a great decision because Noah and I desperately needed some practice. We're both still learning and were definitely a little rusty, but we did our best to get our swings back into shape. Afterward, we headed into Whistler for some pizza. It was certainly nothing like New York pizza, but both of our slices had just come out of the oven, and there is something about perfectly hot, fresh pizza that always hits.

This was originally supposed to be primarily a climbing trip, but it quickly evolved into a golfing, climbing, eating, relaxing, and exploring Whistler trip. That ended up being even better.

We also discovered one of our favorite Canadian phrases at the Airbnb: "predatory towing." For some reason, the wording on the parking warning absolutely killed us, so naturally it deserved a photo.

The next part of the trip was really just Noah and me exploring Whistler. There was so much to see, and we spent a lot of time simply walking around and taking everything in.

We made our way through the Olympic Village because Noah wanted coffee and started noticing the incredible water throughout the area. It had this strange milky, mineral quality that looked completely different from the water we were used to seeing. At first we weren't even sure if it was natural, but we would eventually see more of it the next day.

Pictures 15 through 17 were from walking among the trees around the Olympic Village. You could actually smell the cedar as you walked through, which was amazing and made the whole place feel even more distinctly Pacific Northwest.

Pictures 20 through 24 were this random chair we found in the middle of the neighborhood around our Airbnb. It had signatures and names carved all over it, and naturally we spent way more time with this chair than anyone probably needed to.

We grabbed lunch at a nearby French crêpe restaurant, which was honestly just all right. But this was around the time we established one of the defining jokes of the trip: CAD is free. Everything seemed expensive, but because the prices were in Canadian dollars, we simply decided they weren't real. It was a convenient philosophy as we proceeded to spend considerably more money than we usually did.

That philosophy continued at Scandinave, a beautiful outdoor spa surrounded by the mountains and trees. When we arrived, there was only one massage slot available. I am not as big a fan of massages, so Noah decided to get the massage. The woman at the front desk was incredibly nice and gave me a discount since there weren't any massages available for me.

We spent probably three hours there. I went back and forth between the sauna and cold plunge over and over again, relaxed in the solarium, did some yoga, and generally did absolutely nothing for a few hours. It was fantastic.

We ended the night at a steakhouse, where we got steak tartare, beef Wellington, and a salad. The meal was honestly fantastic and continued our emerging tradition of pretending Canadian dollars did not count.

The next morning, Noah and I decided to start the day with an easy two or three mile hike around a nearby lake. The weather was beautiful, so it was impossible to say no. We grabbed coffees and started walking.

One of the coolest parts was seeing the milky, mineral looking water we had noticed the day before meet completely clear water coming from another part of the river system. You could literally see the two different colors of water coming together. Pictures 43 through 45 were from the lake itself, which was another reminder of just how beautiful this part of British Columbia is.

Of course, Noah also somehow steered us onto the black diamond equivalent route to the lake instead of the green run. The trails were really marked for mountain bikers, so the steepness was very much a factor. Yes, it was quicker, but we were completely out of breath by the time we got to the top. Classic Noah, always taking the most extreme path.

After the hike, we headed back and finally met up with Danny and Sabs for lunch at the Four Seasons. Sabs and I were joking that trips with both Danny and Noah were becoming increasingly more and more lux. We were developing a taste for the finer things in life.

More importantly, it was really nice to actually spend quality time with Danny and Sabs. Their wedding had been so busy that I didn't get much time to really catch up with either of them. Here, we could just sit around, eat, have some great cocktails, get a little boozy, and hang out together without anywhere else we needed to be.

That afternoon, it was time for golf at the Fairmont. The course was absolutely beautiful and, surprisingly, wasn't even that expensive. For a little over $100, we got to play an incredibly well maintained course surrounded by the mountains.

I had to rent clubs and grabbed a few extra sleeves of balls, which turned out to be a very good decision. I definitely did not play particularly well, but the vibes were great the entire time.

One funny memory from warming up was challenging Danny to a putting competition and somehow draining a 30 foot putt right off the bat. I still can't believe that went in.

Our fourth was a random Japanese man named Masa, who turned out to be by far the best golfer in the group. He was incredibly patient and nice about playing with the rest of us and only really blew up on one hole. At some point, he even invited me to play another round with him the next day. I eventually declined, but it became a funny running joke between Danny, Noah, and me.

We had a 4:55 tee time and were determined to get every possible dollar of value out of it, so we kept playing until it was basically pitch black. We somehow made it through the 17th hole, although I lost my ball because I literally couldn't see where it went. At that point, we finally accepted defeat.

Dinner that night was at Bearfoot Bistro, one of the restaurants all the Seattle people had been talking about and one of the nicest restaurants in Whistler. We went all in on the experience, including the vodka tasting room and the full coursed out dinner.

While the boys were having fun doing the ice room vodka tasting, Sabs and I were having the real fun taking photos, exploring all the different vodka bottles, and acting like bartenders.

The food was fantastic, the desserts were great, and the whole meal felt appropriately over the top for a trip where we had already decided money wasn't real.

Then, halfway through dinner, the power went out. Their payment system stopped working, so the restaurant took down our credit card information manually. For at least a little while, there was a chance that our nearly $200 per person dinner might somehow end up being free. Given the theme of the trip, it felt strangely appropriate.

The next morning, Noah and I got ready to head toward Squamish to climb. We started the day with an Australian style meat pie, and I got a mushroom and beef one that was genuinely one of the best pot pies I've ever had.

It had rained that morning, so a lot of the climbing routes were too wet, but thankfully we found a few that were climbable. I was able to flash both a 10B and a 10D while leading, which I was really proud of. The 10B was pretty airy, while the 10D was shorter and much more bouldery. Being able to send both without falling felt really good.

Noah and I were also reminded that we needed to keep practicing our lead climbing technique. We caught ourselves back clipping a few times, which was a good reminder to slow down, pay attention, and continue being careful. But overall, we were both sending hard and having a great day.

One of the funniest memories was all the caterpillars and worms in the trees around the climbing area. Noah became convinced that they were dangerous and that their webs or droppings might be poisonous. We all started freaking ourselves out, only to eventually realize that he had confused them with an entirely different species from the UK and that the ones around us were harmless. We spent part of the drive back reading descriptions of worms and caterpillars and laughing about how worked up we had gotten.

Driving through Squamish also gave us incredible views of the Chief, the enormous granite wall towering over the area. Between the climbing and simply seeing formations like that along the highway, Squamish was a pretty spectacular place to spend the day.

We had originally hoped to see Danny and Sabs again afterward, but between climbing and traffic, we weren't able to make it happen.

Before heading all the way back to Seattle, we stopped in Vancouver for dinner, where Noah picked Saku, an incredible katsu spot he had been to before.

Oh my gosh, it was so good. There was unlimited rice, unlimited soup, great sides, and perfectly fried, fatty katsu. We even ordered a fried cheese dish that I had never tried before. Noah and I were basically losing our minds over the entire meal.

One of the funniest parts was the cabbage and sauce. They gave us what was essentially a ketchup sized bottle of sauce, and Noah absolutely drenched his cabbage in it. By the end of dinner, he had somehow gone through what felt like half the bottle, which I could not stop laughing about.

Afterward, we were walking around Vancouver and found a water station attached to a fire hydrant. Obviously, we had to drink from it. There was something very funny about having an actual picture of us essentially "drinking from a fire hose."

And because we apparently had not eaten enough already, we ended the night with bingsu. It was this banana and whipped cream creation, and it was ridiculously good. We finished the entire thing while laughing about how much food we had already eaten and the fact that we were somehow continuing to eat.

What a great trip and so many great memories. These are genuinely some of my favorite people in my life. I don't get to spend time with them as often as I would like, but whenever I do, it reminds me how much they care about me and how lucky I am to have such good friends. Between Whistler, Squamish, Vancouver, golf, climbing, way too much food, and our newfound belief that Canadian dollars are free, this was one of those trips I know I'll remember.

Looking forward to the next Seattle summer trip.
