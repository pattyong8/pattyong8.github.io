After diving I went to a nearby Maltese restaurant for lunch and had one of my favorite meals of the trip. I learned that rabbit is a traditional food in Malta, with a history tied to the island and periods when people relied heavily on locally available animals for food. There is also a lot of Italian influence in Maltese cuisine, so there were plenty of rich stews, tomatoes, and familiar Mediterranean flavors. The rabbit was spectacular, genuinely one of the best rabbit dishes I have ever had. The guy working there noticed that I was taking a bunch of photos and notes about the food, so he started explaining the dishes, ingredients, and how everything was prepared, and even brought me a small sample of a pork stew they had, which had a similar base to the rabbit and was also delicious. Picture 13 was the dessert that came at the end of the meal and gave me another chance to try something local. The entire lunch ended up being such a great introduction to Maltese food, and I felt incredibly well taken care of. That night I ended up meeting a guy from a dating app who had grown up in Malta, and he offered to show me around some of the nightlife. We bounced around a few clubs and bars in the area. I cannot exactly say I was immersing myself in authentic Maltese culture because we were mostly surrounded by European tourists, especially Parisians. Apparently Malta is a major party destination for them. The drinking age was also 16, and you could definitely tell that the crowd skewed young. I felt a little out of place, but it was still really fun to experience, and I immediately decided that I needed to bring Parth back once he arrived.

Parth remembered a few places he had visited in Florence with Jeremy and Elliot, including a hilltop viewpoint overlooking the city. We took electric bikes most of the way there and then walked up the final little hill. The view was incredible. You could see practically all of Florence stretched out below us as the sun went down. We were not really in the mood to drink after the wedding, so we grabbed a couple of nonalcoholic drinks and just sat there enjoying the sunset. It was such a simple way to spend our last evening in Italy, but one of the prettiest. For dinner Parth remembered a restaurant he had loved on his previous trip, tucked down a small side street and much more local than many of the restaurants we had passed in the busier parts of Florence. Almost everything was fantastic. The large tortellini and ravioli were to die for. The one miss was the gnocchi. We had not realized it came in a beetroot sauce, and the beet flavor was just way too strong for me. It was not creamy or acidic enough to balance it out. It just tasted very, very beet. Still, two incredible pastas out of three was a pretty good way to end our time in Italy.

On our way home, Parth and I had the option of taking a long layover in Paris, somewhere around 12 to 18 hours, so obviously we took it. We had both been to Paris before, so there was no pressure to run around checking off tourist attractions. We could just spend the day eating, walking around, and revisiting a city we already knew. We started with brunch in one of the LGBTQ neighborhoods at a restaurant covered in umbrellas. I ordered avocado toast with salmon, which sounded great but ended up being surprisingly disappointing. The salmon tasted way too fishy, so I ate about half and gave up.

My mom and Doug had also been in Paris not long before us and had told me about a bakery they loved that had a Taiwanese owner. Pictures 194 and 195 were from our visit there. Parth was nice enough to trek across the city with me to check it out, and it was definitely worth the stop. Paris was also brutally hot that day, and we spent a good portion of our walking route ducking into random stores just for air conditioning. Not every place had it, because Paris, but thankfully enough of the retail stores did. Then came easily my most important stop of the day, Chez Janou. I had gone there during our 2024 Olympics trip and still remembered the duck breast as one of my favorite dishes of all time, and there was no way I was going to have a long layover in Paris and not go back. It was every bit as good as I remembered. The duck breast was massive and perfectly cooked, with incredibly crispy skin. The potatoes were unbelievable, and the red wine sauce was so good that the entire dish still ranks as one of my favorites anywhere. Parth did not even really need to eat, but he was willing to come all the way over with me and hang out while I sat at the bar basically just to eat this one specific duck dish. It sounds like a small thing, but it was genuinely one of those moments that reminded me how caring our friendship is. And that was the end of the trip. What started as Parth inviting me to be his plus one to Freddie's wedding somehow turned into Malta, scuba diving, the Blue Lagoon, Gozo, a wine festival, Bologna, Lamborghini, Pagani, Ferrari, Modena, Calcio Storico, Tuscany, a castle wedding, and one final day eating our way through Paris. Always love going to Europe, and I am very glad we managed to turn an Italy trip for Freddie's wedding into a Malta and Paris trip too.
