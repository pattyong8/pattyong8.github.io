Chile 2026

  • With Scott Street, college, and New York friends
  • March 7th to 12th 2026
  • San Pedro de Atacama and Santiago, Chile

This trip was primarily to celebrate Gustav and Agu's wedding in Santiago. It ended up bringing together two different groups of friends: our San Francisco / Bay Area and college group, along with the New York group we had gotten to know through Quinn and her friends. It was a really fun melding of two groups that otherwise probably would not have traveled together. It was also my first time in South America.

Getting There

There weren't good alternatives out of LaGuardia, and for a while we genuinely didn't know how we were going to make it. At one point, Parth and I even considered abandoning Atacama temporarily and flying somewhere else in South America. We looked at Brazil and Argentina, but ultimately decided we wanted to stick it out because all of our friends were already in Atacama.

Over the course of the journey, we dealt with three plus major delays and two cancellations across both the U.S. and Chile. Somehow, we had managed to choose the itinerary with by far the most travel issues out of everyone.

One of the funniest and most stressful moments happened in Santiago. Our flight to Atacama was canceled, and there was a customer service line with literally hundreds of people in it. We would have been at the very back and likely would have been told to fly the following day. Instead, Parth and I just started calling customer service over and over again. Thankfully, Parth finally got through and somehow got us onto another flight later that evening.

We were ridiculously excited because we were desperately trying to make it to Atacama that night. Everyone had a stargazing tour planned, and we thought we were going to miss it. Even the replacement flight required another layover, but after more than 48 hours of traveling, we finally arrived. Somehow, our timing was perfect. We basically arrived just as the bus was coming to pick everyone up for stargazing. After everything it took to get there, it was absolutely worth it.

Night 1: Stargazing

Our first night in Atacama was a stargazing tour. The sky was incredible, and we had a guide teaching us about the constellations, planets, and everything we were looking at.

There were telescopes set up for us, and this was really my first time getting to look closely at stars and planets through a proper telescope. I had always wanted to do something like this, so I was extremely happy just getting to stand there and take everything in.

It also turned out that Bailey and Jack were fascinated by the stars. The four of us, along with Parth, spent a lot of time around one of the telescopes. We looked at Saturn, major constellations, the Milky Way, and a bunch of other things our guide pointed out. Afterward, we had s'mores, tried a few local Atacama bites and condiments, and drank some Chilean wine before finally going to sleep after an extremely long journey.

Day 2: Atacama Desert

This was our big, fully planned day exploring Atacama. We packed into a Sprinter van with our guide and spent the day driving between different parts of the desert. The landscape felt almost alien. Everything was extremely dry and flat, surrounded by mountains and volcanoes, with very few bodies of water. When there was water, it was often extremely salty. Our guide spent the drives explaining the geography, wildlife, and history of the area.

One of our first major stops was a famous flamingo habitat. We learned that flamingos migrate through this part of Atacama during this time of year, and we were able to see quite a few of them relatively close up. The combination of the flamingos, salt flats, water, mountains, and completely barren landscape was beautiful.

We continued toward some beautiful water formations sitting beneath the nearby mountain peaks. It was an interesting climate because the sun was incredibly strong and the UV index was high, but the actual air was still cold. We were covered in sunscreen while also wearing long sleeves and jackets. The air felt incredibly clean and fresh.

Having all of the San Francisco guys who had lived together back in the same place again was really special. We hugged it out, took a few group photos, and just enjoyed being together. It was one of those quiet little trip moments that meant a lot.

We eventually did a hike out toward another water formation that almost looked like an ocean in the middle of the desert. The water was extremely salty, and the landscape continued to feel unlike anywhere I had been before. We spent a lot of the hike taking turns getting photos of each other and enjoying the views.

Our guides set up a long table directly in front of a local church in a very small town and made us lunch. The setting felt completely random, which made it even more fun. This was definitely not a touristy town. While everyone waited for lunch, I walked around and explored a little. There wasn't much going on, but it was interesting seeing a much more local and quiet part of the region.

Lunch was simple and great: rice, corn and tomato salad, lettuce, meat patties, juice, wine, and a few other things. I was particularly happy with the rice, which was surprisingly buttery and delicious. Thankfully, the table was in the shade, so we could all sit together and relax after being out in the sun.

At this point in the trip, we had become obsessed with Monopoly Deal. We had previously done a tournament of sorts in New York, so naturally we had brought enough cards with us to Chile. We started playing outside while redeeming our welcome drinks, which included beer, juice, and piscos. It was incredibly windy, so we literally had to put rocks on top of the Monopoly Deal cards to keep them from flying away.

Even though the air could be cold, the sun was intense enough that it still felt hot during the day, so we eventually got into the pool as well.

That night we had one of our first proper local meals, and the food was incredible. Parth was willing to split dishes with me, which meant we got to try more things. We had ceviche, a buttery garlic chicken dish with fries, and another chicken dish with fries and a fried egg on top.

The last one was essentially described as a "poor man's plate," and it was divine. The fish was incredibly fresh, the chicken was perfectly cooked, and the sauces were hearty and extremely flavorful. After a full day outside, it was exactly the type of meal we wanted.

Day 3: Geysers + Back to Santiago

We had to wake up at around 5:00 a.m. to drive to the geysers because we wanted to arrive around sunrise, when there is apparently the most geothermal activity. The drive there was ridiculous. It was pitch black, the roads were insanely bumpy, music was blaring, and everyone in the back of the van was getting thrown around. I was starting to feel carsick, so I mostly stayed silent and tried to survive. Meanwhile, Matt was sitting in the front casually reading his book despite the roads, which everyone found impressive.

When we arrived, it was freezing. Everyone had brought plenty of layers except for me. Thankfully, I could occasionally stand near the steam coming off the geothermal pools to warm myself up. The geysers themselves were amazing. I had never seen that many geothermal features concentrated in one place. We walked around, took a bunch of group photos, and watched the steam come up as the sun rose.

On the way back, we passed through a completely different landscape from the dry desert we had been seeing for most of the trip: a wet, grassy area with water, birds, and wildlife. We also saw local animals that looked somewhat like small alpacas or llamas grazing nearby. It was a nice reminder of how varied Atacama can feel even within a single day.

After getting back to our accommodations and checking out, we tried to grab food at a local restaurant before heading to the airport. The food took forever. We kept getting closer and closer to when we needed to leave until eventually we had no choice but to take the food with us. We brought it to the airport and ate there while waiting for the flight. I flew back to Santiago with Matt, Parth, Jack, and Connor.

One of the funniest moments was Connor ordering what he thought was a side salad for himself. It arrived and was basically a salad meant for the entire table. The food was good. The steak was tasty, although not necessarily anything life changing, and I ordered mushrooms and a bunch of other sides. There was simply so much food that I couldn't come close to finishing everything.

Matt decided to turn in after dinner, but Connor, Parth, Jack, and I stayed out and walked around Santiago for a little longer. We ended up having a surprisingly deep conversation about how the trip had been going, how the two different friend groups had been getting along, and generally what was happening in everyone's lives.

It was especially meaningful because these were guys I had lived with for years in San Francisco. We had a lot of life updates to share and a lot to talk about. It eventually became a very heartfelt conversation about how much we all cared about one another. It was one of the quieter moments of the trip, but one that I really appreciated.

Day 4: Free Day in Santiago

Matt and I got up early and started exploring Santiago on foot. Matt likes getting an early start and getting things done, so we covered quite a bit before everyone else was out. We climbed a hill in the middle of the city that had apparently served as some type of fortification hundreds of years ago. From the top, we had a great view over the surrounding part of Santiago.

It wasn't the highest viewpoint in the city, but it was a great way to see the neighborhood. It also looked like people had installed climbing bolts into parts of the rock. There was a beautiful church or religious structure near the top. One funny thing I noticed was that instead of carving initials and hearts into rocks or trees, people had carved them directly into a plant. I had never seen that before.

While Matt and I were walking that morning, we passed some huge, beautiful buildings with architecture that felt almost European or Greco Roman. I walked inside to see what it was and realized it was a school. It was one of the most beautiful school campuses I had ever seen: courtyards, palm trees, sculptures, and this mixture of classical looking architecture and modern spaces. It genuinely made me think that I would have loved studying abroad somewhere like that.

We wandered through several interesting parts of the city, including areas with architecture that felt like replicas or interpretations of Paris and other parts of France. Eventually, Parth, Jack, and Quinn wanted to climb the same hill that Matt and I had done that morning. Matt understandably did not want to climb it twice, so I went back up with Parth, Jack, and Quinn.

Eventually Jack and Quinn headed back. Parth, Matt, and I then found a restaurant / bar in a lively part of Santiago and had a few drinks. The drinks were fantastic, and it was just a great area to sit outside and take in the city.

We happened to be in Santiago for Gabby's birthday, so that night we all dressed up and went to a much nicer restaurant for a birthday dinner. Gabby also had some other friends in town who joined us. Parth and I shared several dishes. One was genuinely one of my favorite lamb dishes I've ever had. It wasn't a traditional solid piece of lamb. It had almost been broken down and reformed, but was cooked like one piece and completely fell apart when you ate it. The sauce was incredible.

We also shared a seafood appetizer with shrimp and baby scallops, followed by a seafood risotto that both Parth and I agreed was fantastic. We finished with churros, which were also great. It was nice having everyone dressed up and sitting together around one long table for Gabby's birthday.

We still weren't ready to go to sleep after dinner. Jack, Parth, Matt, Bailey, and I ended up sitting outside in the hotel's downstairs / lobby area. Bailey somehow convinced the hotel manager to let us buy a few bottles of wine and keep hanging out there. It turned into another simple but great night of drinking, catching up, and spending time together.

Day 5: Gustav + Agu's Wedding

Earlier in the day, Jack, Bailey, Connor, Matt, and I went to explore a nearby mall and grab lunch. We found a good restaurant where I ordered fish served on a bed of fried quinoa. It was incredible. Matt unfortunately wasn't invited to the wedding and was leaving later that day, so it was nice getting another meal and some time together before the rest of us headed to the wedding.

Later, everyone started getting ready. Parth and I both wore tuxes, everyone looked great, and we headed over to another hotel where buses were taking guests to the wedding venue.

The venue was beautiful. The ceremony was held outside underneath these huge, old trees with simple wooden chairs and a really beautiful natural setting. More than half of the ceremony was conducted in Spanish, with portions in English for Gustav's side. I loved that. It made the wedding feel like a real combination of both families and cultures.

It was an emotional ceremony. There were a lot of speeches, plenty of people cried, and it was just a beautiful wedding.

After the ceremony, we moved into cocktail hour. There were great drinks everywhere. I had plenty of Aperol spritzes and sangria, and the hors d'oeuvres were fantastic. They served mini empanadas, which weren't quite as good as the empanadas from the day before, mostly because they were tiny, but they were still delicious. We spent the time drinking, meeting people, and enjoying the venue.

At one point I noticed that they were roasting an entire lamb. I was extremely excited when I realized this was going to become the late night / post wedding food.

We all sat down for dinner. I was seated next to Dino and Rishi, and it was just a great atmosphere having everyone together. The food throughout the wedding was excellent.

The rest of the wedding became one huge party. Gustav and Agu at one point drove away in a really cool motorcycle with a sidecar. Later, everyone packed the dance floor. The primary drink at the bar seemed to become pisco and Coke, so naturally we drank plenty of those.

The music was incredible. There were random wigs people could put on, everyone was dancing, and we spent a lot of time with the same people we had gotten to know during the bachelor party. Gustav was also able to spend quite a bit of time hanging out with us, which was really nice.

I tried a ridiculous amount of food throughout the night, including plenty of desserts from the dessert bar. Eventually, the whole lamb I had seen roasting earlier came out, and it was incredible: extremely tender, succulent, and easily one of my favorite foods from the wedding.

More than anything, the energy on the dance floor was electric. This was genuinely one of the most fun weddings I've been to from a pure party and dance floor perspective. It felt very representative of Gustav and Agu: their personalities, the people they surround themselves with, and how much fun everyone has together. It was a perfect way to end the trip.

Parth and I had a layover in Panama on the way home. As we were landing, I was able to see the Panama Canal from the plane for the first time. After my first trip to South America, it felt like a fitting final sight. I definitely want to come back and see more of the continent.
