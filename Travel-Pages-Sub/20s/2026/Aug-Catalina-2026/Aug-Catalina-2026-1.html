Catalina

  • With Cal-KA Buddies
  • August 28th-30th, 2026
  • Catalina Island, California

Catalina has officially become one of our traditions. This was our third year doing the trip, and Matt and his family were once again so kind to host us. Rishi joined us this year, bringing the group to five, and the vibes, as always, were immaculate. There was lots of mini golf, plenty of drinking, some new activities, and most importantly, a really good opportunity to catch up and spend quality time together.

We arrived in Catalina and, as always, one of our first stops was the grocery store to stock up for the weekend. Connor had taken the lead on food this year, which I was more than happy about since I normally end up cooking. That left the rest of us to focus on the important stuff: alcohol.

This year, I was determined to bring Aperol Spritzes into the Catalina tradition. We grabbed a bottle of Aperol and two bottles of Prosecco, along with everything else we needed for the weekend. I also had to take a picture of the BuzzBalls because my New York friends had become obsessed with them. We initially thought we were buying way too much alcohol, but somehow it ended up being almost exactly the right amount.

When we got back to Matt's, we completely filled the fridge. We also discovered a coconut LaCroix flavor that none of us had tried before, and somehow we all became obsessed with it.

It was the perfect temperature outside, and we were all excited to finally be back in Catalina together, so naturally I immediately opened the Aperol and Prosecco. We sat on the second floor deck drinking Aperol Spritzes, catching up, reminiscing, and talking about what we wanted to do over the weekend. None of us had really eaten that day, so the drinks hit surprisingly hard. It was a pretty great way to officially start the trip.

The guys eventually headed to the beach while I stayed behind to get some work done. By the time I finished and walked down to meet them, they were already getting ready to leave, so I jumped into the water for a little while anyway.

I also used the opportunity to call Riva. We had only recently started dating, and this was actually the first time we had really FaceTimed or talked on the phone outside of seeing each other in person and texting. He had asked before I left if we could call, and sitting by the water in Catalina felt like a pretty perfect opportunity to show him what I was up to and catch up for a bit. His parents were still staying with him, so we didn't talk for very long, but it was a nice little memory from early on.

Then it was time for one of the most important Catalina traditions: mini golf.

Rishi and I obviously had to wear our Hawaiian shirts, and Rishi even had a tiny comb for his mustache, which was ridiculous. We also brought High Noon roadies with us to drink while we played.

Mini golf has somehow become one of the most competitive parts of this trip. Jack, Matt, and I are usually pretty evenly matched, and this first round was no different. I had a pretty bad start, but slowly worked my way back into it. Jack and I went back and forth for most of the course and eventually ended up tied.

There were plenty of great moments along the way. Connor played considerably better than in previous years and was deeply focused on every shot. Rishi was also surprisingly competitive. At one point, three of us managed to get hole in ones on the same hole. Everyone except me was wearing white shoes, so apparently I had missed the group memo. Meanwhile, I was getting destroyed by mosquitoes because I had decided flip flops were appropriate golf attire.

Then there was the long hole, where I once again successfully chipped the ball over the wall, as I seem to do every year. That helped me claw my way back into the competition and ultimately tie Jack. Jack also decided at one point to walk up part of the course to adjust the turf and nearly slipped and ate it, so perhaps something we should avoid next year.

Afterward, we headed back to Matt's and sat around the fire pit with wine and chips. These quieter parts of Catalina are always some of my favorite moments of the trip. We talked about settling into our different cities, our friendships, relationships, the activities we've gotten into, and generally where everyone was in life. We don't all live in the same place anymore, so having a few hours where everyone can just sit around a fire and talk is something I really appreciate.

That night, we had originally hoped to eat at one of the nicer seaside restaurants, but the wait was way too long. Matt and Connor decided to grab pizza, while Jack, Rishi, and I found a surprisingly affordable Mexican spot nearby. I got fajitas with rice and beans, and naturally we ordered a pitcher of margaritas for the table.

Afterward, we met back up with Matt and Connor and headed to a bar where there was a bachelorette party they had started talking to. While we were there, we randomly spotted a Sustainable Shane sticker. Shane is one of our friends with a YouTube channel, so seeing evidence of him out in the wild in Catalina was hilarious.

At some point, I split off with Jack and Rishi, and we ended up sitting by the water and having one of those unexpectedly deep late night conversations. We talked about Rishi's breakup, how he was feeling about his life in San Diego, how Jack was feeling about his own life, relationships, and where everyone was generally headed. It was a really nice moment and one of those conversations that I probably wouldn't remember nearly as well if we weren't all away together.

The next day brought a completely new Catalina activity for us: beach volleyball.

Connor, Jack, and Rishi had all been playing more volleyball in San Diego, and Connor in particular was determined to get a game going. I don't really play volleyball, but it ended up being a ton of fun. Connor taught me how to properly return the ball over the net, while Rishi and Matt worked on timing their jumps for blocks.

We hit the ball over the fence far too many times and had to ask far too many tourists to throw it back to us. But considering none of us were taking it too seriously, we actually had some pretty decent rallies.

It was also a surprisingly meaningful activity for Rishi. He was still being cautious with his knee after surgery and wasn't sure how much he would be able to move. Playing volleyball gave him a chance to test it out, and by the end he seemed considerably more confident in his mobility. Hopefully volleyball becomes another Catalina tradition going forward.

From there, we headed somewhere new for us: Descanso Beach. We traditionally hadn't spent much time on this side of Catalina, which is much more about music, parties, and drinking. It ended up being so much fun.

Matt deserves most of the credit because he had somehow gone over at 6 a.m. to put down towels and claim our spot. We didn't actually arrive until almost 1 p.m., and miraculously no one had touched anything. We ended up with prime beach real estate for the entire afternoon.

There was even an Aperol setup there, which felt exactly right for the weekend, although somehow I didn't actually get an Aperol Spritz.

I brought snorkeling gear and fins and spent some time in the water practicing equalizing my ears. I saw a few eels, plenty of Garibaldi, and a bunch of other fish. It wasn't the best snorkeling spot on Catalina, but I didn't particularly want to go over to the casino snorkeling area by myself, so Descanso did the job.

We also ran into some of Matt and Connor's friends from the night before, who happened to be visiting Catalina the same weekend. While hanging out at the tables, we learned a new card game called Flip 7. It's almost like blackjack, where you keep drawing cards and can eventually bust. We became obsessed with it almost immediately and played it several more times throughout the trip.

After an afternoon at Descanso, everyone was at least a little drunk, which apparently meant it was the perfect time for our second round of mini golf.

This time, there was absolutely no competition for first place. Jack put together one of the best mini golf performances we've seen in all three years of Catalina. He was getting hole in ones everywhere and basically pulled away from the group immediately. There was no catching him.

Matt and I were much closer in the battle for second, although I honestly don't remember who ultimately won that one. Either way, Jack's performance was the story of the round.

Back at the house, we returned to the fire pit for another night of food, drinks, and conversation. This time, Connor and Jack cooked dinner: chicken, broccoli, and Rice-A-Roni. It was simple and so good.

Not having to cook this year was honestly kind of amazing. I could just sit back, relax, eat whatever everyone else made, and focus on being present with the guys.

After dinner, we headed to watch the Abba cover band play near the water. This was our second year in a row getting to see this performance. We somehow timed this perfectly!

The Club and Heading Home

Picture #45
Picture #46

Matt and Connor's friends eventually convinced us to go out to Catalina's one real club. After dinner, we figured we might as well humor them and see what Catalina nightlife had to offer.

The club itself was pretty average, but there was a decent dance floor and good enough music. We didn't stay particularly long, but it was fun simply because it was something we had never done on any of our previous Catalina trips. At one point, Jack and Matt ended up dancing behind this little cage area, which produced some pretty hilarious photos of the two of them looking like they were partying behind bars.

The next morning, we cleaned up the house and said goodbye to Matt, who wasn't taking the ferry back with us.

The ferry ride home ended up being one of my favorite parts of the weekend. Connor and I sat outside and talked basically the entire hour and a half. We talked about what we were working on, what we were excited about, the guy I was dating, and what actually matters in a relationship. It was just really nice to have that uninterrupted time together.

While talking with Connor, we noticed the lead singers of the Abba cover band were sitting right next to us. We said hi for a bit. So cool!

Unfortunately, sitting outside for an hour and a half also meant I got completely sunburnt. My mom was not particularly happy when she saw me afterward, but it was worth it for the conversation.

Three years into this tradition, Catalina continues to be one of those trips I look forward to every year. There are the recurring traditions like mini golf, drinks on the deck, and nights around the fire pit, but each year we also seem to add something new. This time it was Rishi joining, Aperol Spritzes, beach volleyball, Descanso Beach, Flip 7, and even a night at Catalina's one club.

More than anything, though, it's a chance for all of us to slow down and actually catch up. We live in different places and our lives continue to change, but for a few days every year, we get to come back to Catalina, hang out like we always have, and pick up where we left off. The vibes remain immaculate.
